Motional EMF

A conductor moving through a magnetic field makes a voltage: the field pushes the charges inside it sideways. Complete the circuit and a current flows, which feels a braking force. This is an electric generator in its simplest form, and a clear case where Faraday, Lenz and energy conservation all agree.

The intuition

Slide a metal rod to the right through a magnetic field pointing into the page. The charges in the rod move with it, so the magnetic force qv × B pushes the positive charges toward one end and the negative toward the other. That separation creates an EMF between the ends: ε = BLv.

Put the rod on rails with a resistor and you have a circuit: current I = BLv/R flows. But a current-carrying rod in a magnetic field feels a force F = ILB, and (Lenz's law) it opposes the motion. To keep the rod moving at constant speed you have to push with exactly that force, and the work you do equals the heat produced in the resistor: Fv = I²R.

If you let go, the rod slows down exponentially with time constant τ = mR/(B²L²): faster means more EMF, more current, more braking. If you keep pushing with a constant force F, the rod speeds up until the braking force equals F: the terminal speed vT = FR/(B²L²), the same form as a falling object with drag.

The rod is launched with speed v₀ and, if F is not zero, pulled with a constant force. The moving rod acts as a battery with EMF BLv. The current it drives feels a magnetic force that opposes the motion (Lenz's law). With F = 0 the rod coasts to a stop; with F > 0 it speeds up until the forces balance.

Try this

Predict what will happen first. Then set it up and check.

  1. With no applied force, launch the rod at 4 m/s and watch it slow down.

    What you should see

    The current is largest at the start and decays as the rod slows: v and I fall together as e^(−t/τ). The kinetic energy all ends up as heat in the resistor.

  2. Double the magnetic field.

    What you should see

    EMF doubles, current doubles, braking force quadruples (it goes as B²). The rod stops in a quarter of the distance.

  3. Push the rod with 1.0 N from rest.

    What you should see

    It speeds up, but the braking force grows with speed. When it equals the push, the acceleration is zero: a terminal speed v_T = FR/(B²L²) = 12.5 m/s.

  4. Increase the resistance and launch with no force.

    What you should see

    A larger R means a smaller current, a weaker braking force, and a longer coast: the time constant mR/(B²L²) is 4 times longer.

Worked example

A 0.50 m conducting rod slides at 3.0 m/s along frictionless rails connected to a 2.0 Ω resistor, in a uniform 0.40 T field into the page. Find (a) the EMF, (b) the current and its direction, (c) the force needed to keep the rod moving at constant speed, and (d) the power. If the rod then has a mass of 0.10 kg and is released with no applied force, find its time constant.

Try it yourself first, then reveal one step at a time.

  1. EMF. ε = BLv = (0.40)(0.50)(3.0) = 0.60 V.
  2. Current and direction. I = ε/R = 0.60/2.0 = 0.30 A. The flux into the page is growing, so the loop opposes it by making field out of the page: counterclockwise, which is upward through the rod.
  3. Force to move at constant speed. The magnetic force on the rod is F = ILB = (0.30)(0.50)(0.40) = 0.060 N, directed left (opposing the motion), so you must push with 0.060 N to the right.
  4. Power. P = I²R = (0.30)²(2.0) = 0.18 W. Check: the push does work at the rate Fv = (0.060)(3.0) = 0.18 W ✓: all of it becomes heat.
  5. Time constant when released. τ = mR/(B²L²) = (0.10)(2.0)/((0.40)²(0.50)²) = 5.0 s. The speed decays as v = 3.0e−t/5.0, and the rod travels a total of v0τ = 15 m before stopping.

Common mistakes

  • Putting the braking force the wrong way. It always opposes the rod's motion (Lenz's law).
  • Forgetting that the magnetic force depends on v, so the rod's acceleration is not constant.
  • Using B²L²v/R for the power: that is the force. Power is B²L²v²/R.
  • Saying that the rod loses energy to the magnetic field. The magnetic force does no net work on the charges; the energy goes to heat via the current.

Two ways to the same answer

  • Magnetic force on charges: in a rod moving with velocity v through B, charges feel qv × B along the rod until an electric field builds up that balances it: E = vB, so the voltage over length L is BLv.
  • Faraday's law: the area of the loop grows at rate dA/dt = Lv, so |ε| = B(dA/dt) = BLv. Same answer.

Energy and the equation of motion

  • Newton's second law: m dv/dt = F − B²L²v/R. This has the same form as drag, so v = vT + (v0 − vT)e−t/τ with τ = mR/(B²L²).
  • Without a force (F = 0): v = v0e−t/τ and the stopping distance is v0τ.
  • Generator and eddy-current brake: turning the rod into a rotating coil gives a generator; a metal sheet moving through a field makes swirling eddy currents that brake it.
